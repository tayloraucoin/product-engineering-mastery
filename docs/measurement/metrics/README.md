# Metrics templates

**What this is.** Events, metric definitions, experiment plans and readouts, as blank templates. Each is filled at one moment in the work and read at another.

**Come here when.** A package's instrumentation section needs an event, a metric's formula changes, a variant is about to go live, or cool-down asks what shipped.

**Start with.** [`events.template.md`](events.template.md), then [`definitions.template.md`](definitions.template.md).

## A worked scenario: shipping a change behind a flag

Synthetic throughout: the product, accounts and numbers are invented.

A records app adds **bulk archive**: tick several rows in the records table and archive them in one action. Users have asked for it, but nobody knows yet whether it helps them keep the table tidy or just makes accidental archiving easier. The team ships it behind the flag `bulk-archive` and reads whether it worked.

1. **Name the number before building.** In the Frame stage the brief's Metric field needs an ID and a version, so **Tally** fills [`definitions.template.md`](definitions.template.md) first: `M-04 records archived per active account per week, v1`, with "records restored within 24 hours of an archive" as the companion, so a jump in archiving cannot hide a jump in mistakes. The **shaper**, with Compass, then cites `M-04 v1` in the brief.
2. **Name the events.** **Tally** adds two rows to [`events.template.md`](events.template.md): `records_bulk_archived` (properties: count, opaque account ID) and `record_restored` (property: `source`). The builder proposes them in the surface file's Instrumentation section; Tally checks the names against the grammar and the property hygiene rule.
3. **Write the rule before the flag flips.** Six named accounts will get the flag, so the plausibility line says "flagged trial, not a controlled experiment": six accounts cannot show a lift. **Tally** drafts [`experiment.template.md`](experiment.template.md) and the **shaper** signs and dates it: ship if `M-04` rises within each account against its own prior two weeks and restores stay under 5 percent; kill at once if one record the user did not select is archived.
4. **Ship.** On deploy day the **builder** and **Tally** fill the release checklist ([`release.template.md`](../../runbooks/release.template.md)): events verified on seeded data, the rule dated, the flag on for the six accounts. At 48 hours the **shaper** watches replays.
5. **Read it.** At the end of the two-week window **Tally** drafts [`readout.template.md`](readout.template.md) and the **shaper** decides. What moved: four of six accounts used bulk archive; their median went from 3 to 19 records a week; 2 of 212 archives were restored (0.9 percent); replays show people clearing imported duplicates. Two accounts never opened the table in the window, so the result is "ship, as a flagged trial". What this scale cannot tell us: whether it holds for accounts with ten thousand records, and the cheapest way to find out is a cohort rollout once more than ten accounts are active.
6. **Afterwards.** If `M-04` is later redefined, say to count only active records, Tally bumps it to v2 in the definitions file and both series run side by side until v1 is retired in its changelog.

## In this folder

| File | What it is for |
| --- | --- |
| [`definitions.template.md`](definitions.template.md) | Fill when a metric is named in a brief, or when a metric's formula, denominator or window changes; the definition is a public API with a version and a changelog. |
| [`events.template.md`](events.template.md) | Fill before a package's instrumentation section cites an event, or when an event is added, renamed or retired; the names, properties, owners and status of every tracked event. |
| [`experiment.template.md`](experiment.template.md) | Fill before a flag flips for a variant, flagged trial or controlled experiment; the success, kill and stopping rules written before any data exists. |
| [`readout.template.md`](readout.template.md) | Fill in cool-down for what shipped this cycle, and at the close of every variant window; answers two questions in order and stops when nothing moved. |
