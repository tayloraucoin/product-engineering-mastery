# Brand Pillars — The Three-Circle Method

> Dump every word that feels like the brand, cut the ones anyone could claim, cluster the rest into three circles, and let everything else flow downstream from them.

**Verdict:** ✅ The core method (diverge on attributes → converge to ~3 pillars → cascade into identity) matches how branding is widely practiced. 🟡 The Venn diagram is a facilitation device, not a canonical framework — but it's a good one, and it gets better if you read the overlaps, not just the circles.

**Doc type:** Protocol · **Origin:** Taylor's first day at his first dev job (Dec 2016), watching a senior exec guide a rebrand on a whiteboard.

---

## TL;DR

1. **Ground first.** Before the whiteboard, answer: why do we exist, who is it for, who are we up against. Pillars without these float.
2. **Diverge.** Everyone silently writes characteristic words. Get them all on the board. No debate.
3. **Cut table stakes.** Delete every word nobody would ever claim the opposite of (honest, integrity, quality, passionate). They're entry fees, not identity.
4. **Cluster into three circles.** Group what's left. Name each circle. Those are the pillars.
5. **Read the overlaps.** Each pairwise overlap is a signature behaviour; the centre is the brand essence (one line).
6. **Rank and guardrail.** Pick the one dominant pillar. Define each as "X but not Y."
7. **Translate.** Run every pillar through color, type, voice, logo, imagery, UI, motion. Anything that doesn't trace back to a pillar is decoration.
8. **Govern.** Use the pillars as a yes/no filter for every future decision.

---

## Why care

A brand is a set of thousands of small decisions — a button label, a shade of green, which partner to say yes to. Without pillars, each decision gets made by whoever's in the room, by taste, and the brand slowly becomes incoherent. With pillars, every decision has a reference point, a designer and a copywriter who never meet produce work that feels like the same entity, and an AI given the pillars produces on-brand output instead of generic output.

For Taylor specifically: this is the upstream artifact for every venture (Agora, CC, Starlight, Weave, client sites). Do it once per brand, then paste it into any thread that touches design, copy, or UI.

## The mental model

**A brand is a tree.**

| Layer           | What it is                                               | Examples                                                     | Changes how often        |
| --------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------ |
| 🌱 **Roots**    | Why it exists, who it's for, where it sits in the market | Purpose, audience, competitors, 10-year vision               | Almost never             |
| 🪵 **Trunk**    | The three pillars — the brand's character                | "Playful · Grounded · Fierce"                                | Rarely (rebrands)        |
| 🌿 **Branches** | Identity systems derived from the trunk                  | Color, typography, logo, voice, imagery, motion, UI patterns | Occasionally (refreshes) |
| 🍃 **Leaves**   | Individual touchpoints                                   | A landing page, an email, a sign, an onboarding screen       | Constantly               |

The whiteboard method Taylor learned builds the trunk. The main enhancement from research: **the trunk needs roots.** Pillars chosen without purpose, audience, and competitive context tend to be generic, because they describe what the team wants to be rather than what makes it distinct _to someone, against someone_.

**The Venn is a clustering machine.** Mechanically, the three-circle step is affinity mapping — grouping related words until patterns emerge — with a hard constraint of three groups. The constraint is the point: it forces choices.

**The Venn's hidden layer is the overlaps.** Most people stop at the three circle labels. The richer output is:

```
            ┌─────────┐
            │ Pillar A │
       ┌────┼────┐    │
       │ A∩C│ ★  │A∩B │      ★ = Essence (one line)
       │    └────┼────┘      A∩B, B∩C, A∩C = signature behaviours
       │ Pillar C│ B∩C│      — where two traits combine into
       └─────────┼────┘        something only this brand does
                 │Pillar B
```

- **Circles** = the three traits (what we are).
- **Pairwise overlaps** = signature expressions (how two traits combine into a specific behaviour, e.g. _Playful ∩ Fierce_ → "try hard, laugh harder").
- **Centre** = the brand essence — the single idea that holds all three.

_(The overlap reading is a synthesis, not an industry-standard step. It's worth doing because it turns three adjectives into concrete behaviours.)_

## The details — the protocol

### Step 0 · Ground (the roots)

Answer these before touching attribute words. Short answers are fine.

| Question                                                                 | Why it matters                                                           |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Why do we exist?** (What → How → Why, Sinek's Golden Circle)           | Pillars should express the why, not just the vibe                        |
| **Who are our top 1–2 audiences?**                                       | A pillar is only valuable if _they_ value it                             |
| **Who are the 3–5 alternatives people choose instead?**                  | You can't differentiate from nothing                                     |
| **Where are we in 5, 10, 20 years?**                                     | Prevents pillars that fit today's product but not the company            |
| **What do customers already say about us?** (reviews, testimonials, DMs) | Outside-in check; a brand is what people say when you're not in the room |

This mirrors the first half of GV's Three-Hour Brand Sprint (20-year roadmap, What/How/Why, top audiences, competitive landscape).

### Step 1 · Diverge (the brain dump)

- Everyone writes words **silently and separately** first, then reads them out and they all go on the board. (GV's "note-and-vote" rule: open discussion at this stage lets the loudest voice win.)
- Anything goes: adjectives, values, feelings, "if we were a person we'd be…"
- Aim for 30–80 words.
- Expect the list to mix four kinds of words — fine for now, but notice them:

| Kind                               | Examples                       | Where it ends up                    |
| ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------ | ----------------------------------- |
| **Character** (how we come across) | playful, precise, warm, bold   | → Pillars (the main target)         |
| **Values** (what we believe/do)    | transparency, craft, community | → Pillars or a separate values list |
| **Tone** (how we speak)            | witty, plainspoken, direct     | → Voice guidelines (downstream)     |
| **Presentation** (how we look)     | minimal, vibrant, textured     | → Visual identity (downstream)      |

### Step 2 · Cut the table stakes ✂️

This is the single biggest enhancement to the original method. Words like _honest, integrity, quality, trustworthy, passionate, innovative, customer-centric_ are **permission-to-play** — every brand is expected to have them, so they differentiate nothing.

Two tests:

- **The Opposite Test:** Would any sane brand claim the opposite? Nobody claims "dishonest," so _honest_ goes. But brands genuinely choose "formal" vs "casual," "exclusive" vs "open," "playful" vs "serious" — those are real choices and they stay.
- **The Competitor Test:** Could your top competitor put this word on their site without it being false? If yes, it's not yours.

Rule of thumb: **show table stakes, don't state them.** You prove honesty through behaviour; you don't make it a pillar.

Exception: a table-stakes word can survive if you give it a specific, ownable meaning (e.g. "integrity" meaning _structural robustness_ for an engineering firm).

### Step 3 · Cluster into three circles

- Draw three overlapping circles.
- Place words by feel; related words drift together. Within a few minutes, groups emerge.
- **Name each circle with one word** — the most precise word in the cluster, or a new word that captures it. The other words become that pillar's supporting attributes.
- If you have four strong groups, you have two options: merge the closest pair, or accept four and drop the Venn. Industry practice ranges 3–5 pillars; three is the sweet spot for memorability and it's what the Venn enforces.

### Step 4 · Read the overlaps

For each pair, ask: _what does this brand do when both traits are active at once?_ Write one phrase per overlap.
Then the centre: _what single idea holds all three?_ One line. This becomes the brand essence / north star and often seeds the tagline.

### Step 5 · Rank and guardrail

- **Rank:** Choose the one dominant pillar. When two pillars conflict in a real decision, the dominant one wins. (GV explicitly requires a single most-important value; this is where teams stall, and it's the most important decision.)
- **Guardrail:** Define each pillar as **"X but not Y"** — naming the failure mode of the trait overdone. Mailchimp's classic voice list works this way ("fun but not childish," "expert but not bossy"). This prevents a pillar from sliding into its caricature.
- **Calibrate intensity (optional):** Personality sliders — place the brand on spectra like _Classic ↔ Modern, Expressive ↔ Reserved, Friend ↔ Authority, Mass ↔ Elite_. Plot competitors on the same axes; the empty space is often where you want to be.

### Step 6 · Translate downstream (branches)

Run every pillar through every system. Fill this table — if a cell is empty, that system has no reason to look the way it does.

| System                                      | Pillar A | Pillar B | Pillar C |
| ------------------------------------------- | -------- | -------- | -------- |
| Color                                       |          |          |          |
| Typography                                  |          |          |          |
| Logo / mark                                 |          |          |          |
| Imagery & illustration                      |          |          |          |
| Voice & copy                                |          |          |          |
| UI patterns & interaction                   |          |          |          |
| Motion & sound                              |          |          |          |
| Behaviour (policies, service, partnerships) |          |          |          |

Useful heuristics, mapped to Aaker's five brand-personality dimensions (the one empirically validated personality model in branding):

| If a pillar leans…                              | Visual tendencies                                             | Voice tendencies                        |
| ----------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| **Sincerity** (warm, genuine, down-to-earth)    | Warm hues, rounded forms, humanist type, real photography     | Conversational, plain, first-person     |
| **Excitement** (daring, spirited, imaginative)  | Saturated color, high contrast, heavy weights, lots of motion | Short, punchy, playful, surprising      |
| **Competence** (reliable, intelligent, precise) | Restrained palette, strict grid, geometric/grotesque sans     | Clear, confident, specific, data-backed |
| **Sophistication** (refined, elegant)           | Neutrals, generous whitespace, high-contrast serif            | Few words, understated, never shouting  |
| **Ruggedness** (tough, outdoorsy)               | Earth tones, texture, slab serifs, grit                       | Blunt, plainspoken, physical verbs      |

🟡 These are conventions, not laws — breaking one deliberately can be the most distinctive move you make. Use them as defaults to argue against.

### Step 7 · Govern (use it or lose it)

- Every design review, copy review, partnership, and feature decision asks: **"Which pillar does this express? Does it violate any 'but not'?"**
- Put the pillar card (template below) at the top of every brand/design/copy thread and file.
- Revisit only at real triggers: new audience, new category, merger, or the brand has visibly outgrown the pillars.

### Worked example (fictional: _Tidepool_, a neighbourhood climbing gym)

- **Brain dump (partial):** safe, clean, friendly, goofy, gritty, local, community, challenging, curious, unpretentious, ambitious, quality, experimental, welcoming, strong.
- **Table-stakes cut:** safe, clean, friendly, quality, welcoming → gone (every gym claims them; prove them instead).
- **Circles:** **Playful** (goofy, curious, experimental) · **Grounded** (local, community, unpretentious) · **Fierce** (challenging, gritty, ambitious, strong).
- **Overlaps:** Playful ∩ Grounded → _neighbourhood clubhouse_ · Grounded ∩ Fierce → _earned, not bought_ · Playful ∩ Fierce → _try hard, laugh harder_.
- **Essence:** _Where effort becomes play._
- **Dominant pillar:** Fierce.
- **Guardrails:** Playful but not childish · Grounded but not plain · Fierce but not exclusive.
- **Translation snippets:** chalky textured backgrounds + one loud accent color (Fierce/Playful); hand-set route names in the app (Playful); no pro-athlete stock photos, only members (Grounded); onboarding copy "Everyone falls. That's the point." (Playful ∩ Fierce).

## Real vs. hype

| Claim                                                                                  | Rating | Notes                                                                                                                                    |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Brands should be built on a small set of core pillars that drive everything downstream | ✅     | Near-universal in practice across agencies and frameworks                                                                                |
| Three is the right number                                                              | 🟡     | Common range is 3–5; three is a strong default, not a rule                                                                               |
| Start with a big attribute dump, then narrow                                           | ✅     | Standard divergent → convergent workshop pattern (GV Brand Sprint, affinity mapping)                                                     |
| The Venn diagram is _the_ branding method                                              | 🟠     | It's one facilitation format among many (sliders, radar charts, archetype maps, prisms). Its strength is the forced three-way constraint |
| Words like "honest" and "integrity" make good pillars                                  | ❌     | Widely criticized as table stakes — expected by default, differentiating nothing                                                         |
| Brand personality is a real, measurable thing                                          | ✅     | Aaker (1997, _Journal of Marketing Research_) found five robust dimensions, analogous to the Big Five in human personality               |
| Specific stats like "brand affinity increases conversions 131%"                        | ❔     | Common in marketing blogs, usually untraceable; don't repeat them                                                                        |
| Pillars alone are a brand strategy                                                     | ❌     | Pillars are the trunk. Without purpose, audience, and positioning (roots), they drift generic                                            |

## How to use it

### Pillar card (the output — paste this at the top of any brand thread)

```markdown
# [Brand] — Pillar Card

**Essence:** [one line]
**Purpose (why):** [one sentence]
**Primary audience:** [who]
**Main alternatives:** [3–5 competitors / substitutes]

## Pillars (ranked)

1. **[Pillar A]** (dominant) — [A] but not [anti-A]. Supporting: [3–4 words]
2. **[Pillar B]** — [B] but not [anti-B]. Supporting: [3–4 words]
3. **[Pillar C]** — [C] but not [anti-C]. Supporting: [3–4 words]

## Overlaps (signature behaviours)

- A ∩ B: [phrase]
- B ∩ C: [phrase]
- A ∩ C: [phrase]

## Sliders

Classic ●───── Modern · Expressive ───●── Reserved · Friend ─●──── Authority · Mass ────●─ Elite

## Explicitly NOT us

[3–5 words or references we reject]
```

### Where the card goes in a product repo

The filled card is saved as `apps/<app>/docs/design/brand-pillars.md`, beside the product's design layer, and attached to every brand, design and copy thread. The Step 6 table is then made real in the files listed in [`branding.md`](branding.md): each colour, typeface and voice rule lands in one named file with a check. The branding thread that guide prints runs this protocol as its interview.

### Running it solo (founder + AI)

The group version relies on multiple people diverging independently. Solo, you lose that, so compensate:

1. Do Step 0 yourself, then **mine outside words**: testimonials, reviews, how friends describe the product, DMs.
2. Brain dump your own words first, _before_ asking AI (so AI doesn't anchor you).
3. Then ask AI to add words from the perspective of each audience and each competitor's customer.
4. Do the table-stakes cut and clustering yourself. AI is a good sparring partner for the overlaps and "but not" guardrails.
5. Sleep on the ranking. Pick the dominant pillar the next day.

### Prompt for other threads

```
Venue: Claude Code in the product's repo when the result lands in files; a general Claude thread to think it through first.
Model: the deepest available. A smaller model accepts generic words.

Run the Brand Pillars — Three-Circle Method on [brand].
Context: [what it is, who it's for, competitors, any existing words/testimonials].
Follow the protocol: ground (Step 0) → brain dump → table-stakes cut (Opposite + Competitor tests)
→ cluster into 3 named pillars → overlaps + essence → rank + "X but not Y" guardrails → sliders.
Ask me for anything from Step 0 you can't infer. Challenge generic words.
Output: the filled Pillar Card, then the Step 6 translation table.
```

For downstream work (logo, UI, copy) in any thread: paste the Pillar Card and say _"Every choice must trace to a pillar; flag anything that violates a 'but not'."_

## Say it out loud

"You start by throwing every word that feels like the brand onto a whiteboard. Then you cross out the ones every company would say — honest, quality, integrity — because those are just the entry fee. What's left, you cluster into three circles, and each circle becomes a pillar. The cool part is the overlaps: where two pillars meet is where the brand's signature behaviour lives, and the middle is the one idea that holds it all. Pick which pillar wins in a tie, write each one as 'this but not that,' and then every color, font, and sentence has to trace back to one of them."

<summary>Related frameworks worth knowing</summary>

- **GV Three-Hour Brand Sprint** (Jake Knapp, Laura Melahn, Daniel Burka): six exercises — 20-year roadmap, What/How/Why, top 3 values, top 3 audiences, personality sliders, competitive 2×2 (Classic↔Modern × Expressive↔Reserved). Closest published cousin of Taylor's method; forces top three values with one ranked first. https://library.gv.com/the-three-hour-brand-sprint-3ccabf4b768a
- **Aaker's Brand Personality Dimensions** (1997): Sincerity, Excitement, Competence, Sophistication, Ruggedness, each with facets. The empirical backbone for "brands have personalities." https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/002224379703400304
- **Simon Sinek's Golden Circle** (Why → How → What): the root layer.
- **Brand archetypes** (12 Jungian archetypes — Explorer, Sage, Rebel, etc.): an alternative lens for character; useful when pillars feel abstract. Mix of real narrative craft and pop-psychology; treat as 🟡.
- **Kapferer's Brand Identity Prism, Keller's Brand Pyramid:** heavier academic models covering the same territory; useful for large brands, overkill for startups.
- **Mailchimp voice ("X but not Y")**: the canonical guardrail format. https://styleguide.mailchimp.com/voice-and-tone/
