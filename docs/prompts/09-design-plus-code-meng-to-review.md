# Prompt 09 — Design+Code (Meng To): worth it in 2026, and how much is teaching?

**Model:** Opus
**Inject:** Vesper (Lead UX/UI Designer) reviews the craft; the verdict is addressed to Plumb.
**Attach:** `00-shared-context`, the Vesper role prompt, textbook Part 8.1 (the Design+Code row, rated "solid; quality varies").
**Expected output:** an in-depth review with the same three questions as the Refactoring UI review — holds up, worth it in the AI era, teaching versus tips — plus a specific read on its pivot to "Prompt Better User Interfaces and Apps," ending in a canon ruling and a purchase verdict.

---

Vesper — you're on this one. Read your role prompt and the shared context first.

**The decision this serves.** Design+Code (designcode.io; Pro $349/yr or $99/mo; Lifetime $499) has repositioned itself around prompting better UI and apps, which is exactly the gap I'm closing — but the textbook's read was "AI-building breadth; quality varies," and the catalogue is large and uneven. I want to know what is in there, which courses are real teaching versus tool tours, whether Meng To's design-engineering sensibility (the SwiftUI and Framer era) survives the pivot, and whether any of it should enter my canon or my study plan.

**The ask.**

1. The catalogue, inventoried from designcode.io: every course and its stated outline, length, date published or last updated, the tool or stack it assumes, and whether it is design, code, or prompting. Mark stale courses (tools that no longer exist or have changed materially) by name. `[TITLE ONLY]` where no outline is public.
2. The pivot: what "Prompt Better User Interfaces and Apps" actually contains, from the course pages and any free previews — the workflow it teaches, the tools it assumes (Cursor, Claude Code, v0, Figma Make, others), the design principles it carries into the prompting, and whether it teaches a verify-and-critique loop or only generation. Compare its workflow to Recipe A and say where they agree and where his is weaker or stronger.
3. Meng To's own point of view: from his public writing, talks, and X posts, the design-engineering principles he holds (his views on motion, gradients, glass and depth, UI aesthetics), and whether those principles match or conflict with my design layer's restraint rules and the slop tells I've banned. Be honest if his aesthetic is the one my anti-patterns file rejects.
4. Does it hold up, and is it worth it in the AI era? Same test as the Refactoring UI review: which content is timeless craft, which is tool-specific and perishable, which an agent already does for me. Where does it sit relative to Shift Nudge, Refactoring UI, and the free material?
5. Teaching versus tips: which courses build a mental model versus walk through a tool. Estimate hours to value for a product engineer with my background.
6. The canon ruling, addressed to Plumb: any principles worth adopting (in principle / example / counter-example form), any prompting techniques worth folding into the UI prompting checklist, and the purchase verdict — Pro, one month of Pro to mine specific courses (name them), or skip.

**Evidence rules.** designcode.io and Meng To's own posts are primary. Reviews are secondary and named. Prices dated. Do not describe a course from its marketing sentence; use the outline and previews.

**Output shape.** Verdict; the catalogue table; the pivot analysis with a Recipe A comparison table; the point-of-view section; the holds-up analysis; the teaching assessment with hours; the canon ruling and purchase verdict; sources with dates. Run your drift and register tests on anything you propose for the canon.

**Done means** I know whether to spend a month on it and which two or three courses to mine if so, and Plumb has the candidates.

**Not wanted:** a catalogue with no judgment, or a verdict that ignores the aesthetic conflict question.
