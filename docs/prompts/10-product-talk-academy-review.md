# Prompt 10 — Product Talk Academy (Teresa Torres): is the program alive, current, and worth it?

**Model:** Opus
**Inject:** Envoy (User Research Specialist) — discovery is Envoy's trade; Compass is consulted for whether the method fits our stage.
**Attach:** `00-shared-context`, the Envoy role prompt (§7 filled for DealReady if you have it), textbook Part 6 and Part 8.1 (the Product Talk row).
**Expected output:** an investigation report that answers whether the academy is current and active behind its dated website, what the program actually teaches beyond the book, and whether to enrol — ending in a discovery-practice ruling for our two products.

---

Envoy — you're on this one. Read your role prompt and the shared context first.

**The decision this serves.** Continuous Discovery Habits is the discovery method I'm adopting — weekly customer touchpoints, the Opportunity Solution Tree, assumption tests over idea tests — and Product Talk Academy is the canonical training for it. But the website looks years old, and an outdated site on a program that sells "continuous" anything is a red flag I want investigated, not explained away. I need to know if the program is current, what it teaches that the book doesn't, whether it has absorbed AI-era practice, and whether it's the right spend versus a Maven cohort or self-training from the book.

**The ask.**

1. Is it alive? Verify from primary sources with dates: current course listings and next cohort dates, recent alumni activity, Torres's own recent output (the newsletter, the podcast "Just Now Possible," her 2025–2026 posts and talks), the 5th-anniversary book club, and any pricing you can find. If the site is stale but the program is active, say where the activity actually lives. If the program has thinned, say so plainly.
2. What it teaches beyond the book: the Discovery Fundamentals format (six weekly two-hour live sessions per the textbook — verify), the deep dives, the exercises and the feedback model, and the specific skills the cohort builds that a reader of the book doesn't get. Quote the syllabus where public. `[NOT IN SOURCE]` for paid content.
3. Has the method absorbed AI? What Torres has said publicly about AI in discovery — synthesis, interview analysis, prototyping, the risk of synthetic users — and whether the academy's material reflects it. Where it hasn't, say what I'd have to add myself (Alembic's synthesis discipline, Recipe A's prototyping speed).
4. Fit for our stage: run your decision-first intake on the question. What decision does enrolling inform, what would change it, and what's the cheapest way to get the skill — the book plus disciplined practice, a Maven discovery cohort, Product Talk itself, or coaching. Compare cost, hours, and what each produces.
5. The discovery-practice ruling for DealReady (three investor conversations a week, an Opportunity Solution Tree from the start) and Fybr (two operator or surveyor conversations a week plus onboarding replays): the cadence, the interview guide shape, the tree's outcome node, and the assumption-test types that fit each — in your own method's terms, with what Product Talk would add if I enrol.

**Evidence rules.** producttalk.org, learn.producttalk.org, Torres's newsletter and podcast are primary and dated. Reviews and alumni posts are secondary and named. Don't infer program health from the site's design; find the activity or its absence.

**Output shape.** Verdict; the liveness evidence with dates; the program content beyond the book; the AI question; the fit ruling with the cost comparison as a table; the discovery-practice ruling per product; sources.

**Done means** I know whether to enrol and, either way, I have the discovery cadence and the interview-guide shape for both products.

**Not wanted:** a summary of Continuous Discovery Habits (I've read it), or deference to Torres's reputation in place of evidence about the program's current state.
